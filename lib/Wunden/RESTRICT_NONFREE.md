# Restricting a non-free resolution using left cosets

[restrict_nonfree.gi](restrict_nonfree.gi) supplies:

```gap
LoadPackage("hap");
S := RestrictNonFreeResolution(R,K);;
```

Here `R` is a `HapNonFreeResolution` for `G := R!.group`, and `K` is a
**finite-index subgroup of G**, represented by elements of the same ambient
group. The output is a `HapNonFreeResolution` for `K` on the same underlying
chain complex. HAP and its dependencies must be available.

For a building constructed earlier, the call is simply

```gap
# K is your finite-index subgroup of B63!.group.
S := RestrictNonFreeResolution(B63,K);;
List([0..Length(S)],S!.dimension);
S!.boundary(1,1);
S!.stabilizer(1,1);
```

The implementation is separate from the building constructors and does not
change `buildin.g`, `building_general.g`, or `CONSTRUCTION.md`.

## 1. Why changing `R!.group` is not enough

Suppose an original representative cell is $\sigma$, with stabilizer
$H=\operatorname{Stab}_G(\sigma)$. Its translates form one $G$-orbit, but
may form several $K$-orbits. Each new orbit needs a representative, a new
stabilizer, and boundaries expressed in the new representatives.

For example, let $S_3$ act on a filled triangle. There is one orbit in each
dimension. Restricting to the subgroup generated by a transposition gives
two vertex orbits, two edge orbits, and one triangle orbit. Restricting
instead to the trivial subgroup gives three vertices, three edges, and
one triangle. The underlying triangle is the same in each case.

In general the new cell counts are neither the old counts nor necessarily
the old counts multiplied by $[G:K]$.

## 2. Left cosets throughout

The procedure enumerates the finite set of **left cosets**

$$
G/K=\{xK:x\in G\}.
$$

The list `S!.cosetRepresentatives` stores matrices or group elements $x$,
not coset objects. To recognize a coset, the code uses

$$
yK=xK\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^{-1}y\in K.
$$

Enumeration starts with $K$ and repeatedly applies the generators of $G$
by **left multiplication**: $xK\mapsto gxK$. Only subgroup membership
and group arithmetic are needed. Positive generators suffice because
they act as permutations of a finite coset set, so their inverse actions
are positive powers.

For an original stabilizer $H$, the code then computes its orbits on
$G/K$, again by left multiplication. An orbit represented by $xK$
corresponds to the new cell representative

$$
\tau=x^{-1}\sigma.
$$

The inverse here is essential. If $yK=hxK$ for $h\in H$, then
$y=hxk$ for some $k\in K$, and
$y^{-1}\sigma=k^{-1}x^{-1}\sigma$ up to the orientation sign of $h$.
Thus the two cosets give cells in the same $K$-orbit. Conversely, cells
in the same $K$-orbit give cosets in the same $H$-orbit.

Each original cell can split into a different number of new orbits.
The code records the new indices explicitly rather than assuming a common
number of translates for all original cells.

## 3. Stabilizers via Schreier's lemma

The new cell stabilizer is

$$
J=\operatorname{Stab}_K(x^{-1}\sigma)
 =K\cap x^{-1}Hx.
$$

It is unnecessary to ask GAP to intersect two infinite groups. Instead,
Schreier's lemma computes generators from the finite $H$-orbit of $xK$.

For every visited coset $T_qK$, the code stores a witness $w_q\in H$ with

$$
w_qxK=T_qK.
$$

For a generator $a\in H$, suppose $aT_qK=T_rK$. Then

$$
z=w_r^{-1}aw_q
$$

fixes the coset $xK$. These elements generate its stabilizer
$H\cap xKx^{-1}$. Conjugating them by $x^{-1}$ gives generators
$x^{-1}zx$ of the required subgroup $J$.

This works whether $H$ is finite or infinite, provided its generators and
the required membership tests are available. The new stabilizers are actual
GAP subgroups of `K`, with explicit generators.

An attribute `RestrictionCellStabilizerData` stores $K$, $H$, and the
translate $t=x^{-1}$. The installed membership method then uses the exact test

$$
g\in J\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
g\in K\ \text{and}\ t^{-1}gt\in H.
$$

As in the building code, `DeclareAttribute` supplies the getter, setter and
tester, while `InstallMethod` provides membership for groups carrying that
attribute. This avoids having to rediscover a membership algorithm for each
new infinite matrix subgroup.

## 4. Rewriting boundaries, including orientations

HAP represents a chain term by `[signedCellIndex,elementIndex]`.
An original term $y\sigma$ must be expressed as a subgroup translate of
one of the new representatives.

Find the left coset $y^{-1}K$. Suppose its orbit has base $xK$ and stored
witness $w$, so that

$$
y^{-1}K=wxK.
$$

Consequently $k=ywx\in K$, and

$$
y=kx^{-1}w^{-1}.
$$

If $\chi_\sigma:H\to\{1,-1\}$ is the original stabilizer's orientation
character, the chain term becomes

$$
y\sigma=\chi_\sigma(w^{-1})\,k\,(x^{-1}\sigma).
$$

The code obtains this sign from `R!.action` evaluated on the stabilizer
element $w^{-1}$. Ignoring this sign would give wrong boundaries for cells
whose stabilizers reverse orientation. The building constructors have
trivial orientation characters, but this restriction procedure does not
assume that.

For the representative $\tau=t\sigma$, compute
$\partial\tau=t\partial\sigma$ and rewrite every resulting term by the
formula above. All group elements in the returned boundary belong to `K`.
The orientation character of $J$ is transported by conjugation:

$$
\chi_\tau(k)=\chi_\sigma(t^{-1}kt),\qquad k\in J.
$$

For elements outside a cell stabilizer, `S!.action` uses the corresponding
conjugated call to the input's orientation function. The character on the
stabilizer is the part needed for the chain-module relations.

## 5. Chain conversion and contracting homotopies

The output includes two conversion functions:

```gap
S!.fromOriginal(n,word);  # R-chain -> S-chain in degree n
S!.toOriginal(n,word);    # S-chain -> R-chain in degree n
```

Both take a list of HAP terms. For example,

```gap
w := S!.toOriginal(1,[[1,1]]);;
S!.fromOriginal(1,w);
```

The resulting words represent the same chain; they need not be identical
lists, since stabilizers give relations between different translated terms.
The routines preserve terms rather than combining all cancellations.

If `R!.homotopy = fail`, the output also has `homotopy = fail`.
If an explicit contracting homotopy is present, it is transported through
these conversions. It remains available in the degrees where the original
homotopy is defined, including its augmentation component if supplied.
No new contracting homotopy is invented.

## 6. Additional output fields

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `S!.originalResolution` | The input R |
| `S!.cosetRepresentatives` | Representatives x of the left cosets xK |
| `S!.restrictionCells[n+1][j]` | Data for the jth new representative in degree n |
| `.originalCell` in that record | Its original cell index |
| `.translate` | The group element t such that the new cell is t times the original |
| `.stabilizer` | Its subgroup J of K |

Cell data are constructed by degree on demand. Call `S!.dimension(n)` before
inspecting `S!.restrictionCells[n+1]` directly. The usual HAP functions
`boundary`, `stabilizer`, and `action` also prepare the needed degree.

The input's recorded length and characteristic are preserved.
`reduced` is conservatively set to false. Geometric fields specific to a
particular constructor are not copied: the new representatives are described
by their original cell and translate instead.

## 7. Preconditions and computation costs

- `K` must actually be a finite-index subgroup of `R!.group`, in the same
  element representation. For finite ambient groups this is checked with
  `IsSubgroup`. For infinite groups it is the caller's precondition.
  Different identity elements are rejected before coset enumeration. In the
  locally installed HAP data, `ContractibleGcomplex("SL3Z")` uses 6-by-6
  matrices, whereas `BruhatTitsBuildingSL(3,p)` uses 3-by-3 matrices. Those
  two objects cannot be combined directly: an explicit representation map
  is needed to transport the subgroup or resolution first. Naming the same
  abstract group does not make the matrix representations interchangeable.
- Membership in `K`, and in the original cell stabilizers, must be computable
  by GAP. The procedure can use specialized membership methods such as those
  already installed for the building stabilizers; it cannot automatically
  solve membership for every arbitrary infinite matrix group.
- The ambient group and original stabilizers must supply finite generating
  lists. The resolution must have a finite recorded length and finitely many
  generators in each recorded degree.
- When a needed element is absent from `R!.elts`, it is appended so the
  original orientation/homotopy functions can refer to it. Existing indices
  are preserved. Thus `R!.elts` may grow; the input's group, boundaries and
  cell orbit data are not replaced.

The initial enumeration visits $m=[G:K]$ left cosets. Coset lookup uses a
linear scan with membership tests, so this implementation is intended for
manageable finite indices. For each original cell, its stabilizer acts on
at most these same $m$ cosets. Finitely many cosets guarantee termination of
the orbit searches, assuming the underlying group operations terminate.
Infinite-index inputs are outside the contract and may enumerate forever.

## 8. Tests

Run [test_restrict_nonfree.g](../../tst/Wunden/test_restrict_nonfree.g):

```sh
gap -q -T -c 'LoadPackage("hap"); ReadPackage("hap", "tst/Wunden/test_restrict_nonfree.g"); QUIT;'
```

The finite tests restrict the triangle's $S_3$ action to the whole group,
the trivial subgroup, the alternating subgroup, and a nonnormal order-two
subgroup. They check orbit counts, actual generated stabilizers, left-coset
uniqueness, orientation signs, boundary transport, boundary squared zero,
and the transported contraction. Repeating restriction checks the case where
different old cells split into different numbers of orbits.

The infinite-group tests restrict the buildings for
$\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/2])$ and
$\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb{Z}[1/3])$ to finite-index congruence subgroups. They
check subgroup membership, lattice preservation, face inclusions, finite
congruence images, and conversion of translated cells.
